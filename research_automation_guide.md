# RESEARCH AUTOMATION GUIDE

## Quick 15-Minute Professor Research Workflow

### Phase 1: Basic Intel (5 minutes)
1. **Google Search**: "[Professor Name] [University] research"
2. **Faculty Page**: Primary research areas, current projects
3. **Lab Website**: Current focus, recent news
4. **Google Scholar**: Top 3 recent papers (last 2 years)

### Phase 2: Deep Dive (7 minutes)
1. **Read 1-2 Recent Paper Abstracts**: Understand current work
2. **Check Lab Publications Page**: See ongoing projects
3. **Twitter/LinkedIn**: Recent thoughts, conference talks
4. **Graduate Student Pages**: What are current students working on?

### Phase 3: Connection Mapping (3 minutes)
1. **Match Keywords**: Their terms → Your experience
2. **Find Gaps**: What haven't they explored that you could contribute?
3. **Identify Unique Angle**: Your perspective they don't have

## Automation Tools You Can Use:

### Browser Extensions:
- **Scholar Alert**: Get notified of new papers
- **Research Rabbit**: Visual paper connections
- **Connected Papers**: Find related work

### Quick Search Templates:
```
"[Professor Name]" site:scholar.google.com
"[Professor Name]" site:arxiv.org
"[Professor Name]" filetype:pdf recent papers
"[Lab Name]" recent publications
```

### Red Flag Phrases to Avoid:
- "I am interested in your work" (too generic)
- "Your research is impressive" (meaningless)
- "I would like to work with you" (doesn't show understanding)
- "I have read your papers" (without mentioning specific ones)

### Green Flag Phrases:
- "Your recent paper on [specific topic] addresses exactly the challenge I faced when..."
- "I'm particularly drawn to your approach of [specific methodology] because..."
- "Your work on [specific area] offers a solution to [specific problem you've encountered]..."

## EMAIL QUALITY CHECKLIST:

### Before Sending, Ask Yourself:
- [ ] Could this email apply to 10 other professors? (If yes, it's too generic)
- [ ] Did I mention a specific recent paper/project?
- [ ] Did I explain WHY their work matters to me personally?
- [ ] Did I offer something unique they don't already have?
- [ ] Did I avoid their outdated research areas?
- [ ] Would they learn something about me they couldn't get from my CV?

### Final Test:
**Show the email to a friend without context. If they can't tell you which professor it's for or what field they work in, rewrite it.**

## Time Investment vs. Response Rate:
- **Generic email (2 min research)**: ~2% response rate
- **Basic personalization (10 min research)**: ~8% response rate  
- **Deep personalization (20 min research)**: ~25% response rate
- **Perfect alignment (30+ min research)**: ~40%+ response rate

**The math is clear: spending 30 minutes on one perfect email beats sending 15 generic ones.**
